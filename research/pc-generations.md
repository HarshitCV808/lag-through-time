# PC generations timeline — candidate game levels

Each level = an era. The player feels the limit of that era, then upgrades. The final level is the Intel AI PC.

## 1981 — The first personal computer (Intel 8088, IBM PC)
- ~4.77 MHz, text-only green screen, floppy disks, no internet.
- Limit: one task at a time, typed commands.

## 1990s — Pentium era (Intel Pentium, 1993) and Windows 95
- Graphics, mouse, CD-ROMs, dial-up internet.
- Limit: slow loading, dial-up, single core.

## 2003 — Intel Centrino (Pentium M) laptops
- Built-in Wi-Fi made laptops truly portable.
- Limit: battery of 2–4 hours, heavy.

## 2006 — Intel Core 2 Duo
- Multi-core: two things at once (music + browsing).
- Limit: still no AI; video editing slow.

## 2008–2022 — Intel Core i3/i5/i7 generations
- Turbo Boost, integrated graphics, SSD era, cloud apps.
- Limit: AI only through the cloud — needs internet, sends your data away, drains battery when run locally on CPU.

## 2023 — Intel Core Ultra (Meteor Lake): first Intel PC with a built-in NPU
- Start of the "AI PC" category: CPU + GPU + NPU.

## 2024 — Core Ultra Series 2 (Lunar Lake): 48 TOPS NPU, Copilot+ PC ready

## 2026 — Core Ultra Series 3 (Panther Lake, 18A): final level
- ~50 TOPS NPU, ~120 TOPS Arc GPU, up to ~180 platform TOPS.
- Private, offline, low-latency AI; up to all-day battery; x86 app/game compatibility.
- See [[intel-ai-pc-facts]] for details and competitor comparison.
